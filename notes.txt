CODE/DESIGN STYLE

- Readability to a human programmer first.
- Discuss and agree on substantive changes before generating significant code.
- Prefer tight diffs for small changes.
- Named types for meaningful values are good; avoid abstractions that merely rename obvious code.
- Specialized refiners can rely on invariants established by the BidRule that invokes them.
- Do not add defensive checks for impossible contexts merely because TypeScript makes them possible.
- Keep declarative conditions declarative where that is clear, but use named appliesWhen/refiner
  functions when the bridge treatment really requires procedural logic.
- Do not build a complex bidding-rule DSL.
- Preserve all genuinely qualifying bids when appropriate; refiners may order alternatives rather
  than forcing a single "correct" call.
- Comments should explain bidding intent and non-obvious implementation choices.
- Existing project style uses TypeScript, /// top-level/function comments and // internal comments.

IMPORTANT CURRENT SEMANTICS / RECENT WORK TO VERIFY

1. priority.rank:
   - higher rank = higher preference/precedence;
   - higher-ranked qualifying RULES appear before lower-ranked ones;
   - rank does NOT normally eliminate lower-ranked legitimate alternatives;
   - equal ranks have no meaningful declaration-order semantics.

2. Within one rule, call order may express preference. A refiner may remove impossible candidates
   and reorder legitimate survivors.

3. Hand points:
   - HandFacts keeps static valuations such as HCP, totalPointsWLength, totalPointsWShortage,
     lengths, etc.
   - totalPoints constraints now explicitly choose valuation when necessary, e.g.
       totalPoints: { min: 13, valuation: 'shortage' }
   - ordinary/default totalPoints means length valuation.
   - supportLength means length in partner's opened suit.
   - supportFor was considered redundant and should have been removed; verify no stale uses remain.
   - supportLength by itself must NOT implicitly decide point valuation.

4. Natural NT openings:
   - natural 1NT/2NT openings have high priority (rank 100).
   - optional one-point 1NT upgrade is specifically for a balanced hand with a 5+ CARD MAJOR,
     not any five-card suit.
   - when a natural NT opening genuinely applies, a convenient-minor opening is not retained as
     a lower-ranked alternative.
   - current implementation should reuse evaluated NT rule results rather than duplicating the NT
     point/shape logic imperatively. The helper was renamed maybeSuppressConvenientMinor and should
     only be invoked in opening-bid evaluation.
   - telemetry is developer-facing; learner-facing explanations belong in the explanation/guidance
     path rather than being smuggled through telemetry.

5. Balanced modeling:
   - balanced retains the exact ordinary balanced shapes.
   - "balanced or semi-balanced" was changed/modelled as at least two cards in every suit, because
     that is the useful bidding condition we need. Verify the final names/code in the ZIP.

6. Strong major support:
   - 13+ with exactly three-card major support has a new-suit-first-then-raise treatment.
   - 13+ with 4+ support has the configured strong-support treatment; Jacoby 2NT is one option.
   - refine13PlusMajorSupportNewSuitFirst handles:
       * normal 4+ new minor;
       * 1H-1S with 4+ spades;
       * 1S-2H with 5+ hearts;
       * if no normal new suit exists, an occasional three-card minor fallback;
       * do not include a three-card minor if a normally qualifying new suit exists.
   - Jacoby 2NT uses totalPoints with shortage valuation and requires 4+ support.

7. 2NT -> natural 4H/4S:
   - only an actually held 6+ major survives;
   - preserve both in the freak case that both qualify;
   - order by HCP contained in each suit;
   - spades breaks an equal suit-HCP tie.

8. One-level new-suit response after a minor:
   The current function should be named approximately:
       refineOneLevelNewSuitAfterMinor

   Verify the fresh implementation, but intended behavior is:

   - only legal one-level new suits held 4+ cards qualify;
   - majors are sorted:
       longer major first;
       equal four-card majors H before S;
       equal five-card-or-longer majors S before H.
   - with convenient minors enabled and any 4+ major available:
       prefer the best-length major(s);
       normally suppress diamonds;
       special treatment after 1C with 6-10 HCP:
         if diamonds are STRICTLY LONGER than the best major, retain 1D behind the major(s)
         as a lower-preference alternative.
   - with convenient minors enabled and no 4+ major:
       after 1C, 1D may be the one-level new-suit response;
       after 1D, this rule may produce nothing and another rule such as 1NT can apply.
   - with convenient minors disabled:
       use longest-suit treatment;
       among equally long suits preserve the established major ordering and put an equal-length
       1D after the majors.
   - Do not create a bidding-system setting just for whether the special longer-diamond alternative
     is retained.
